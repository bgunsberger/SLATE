# SlateCheck Assessment Checklist Specification

Version 0.2.0 — working specification

## 1. Purpose

An assessment checklist answers a practical question:

> For this use case, what do we need to find out, approve or complete next?

The checklist is a working view. It may be prepared manually or generated by software. The Proposed Use, decision, approval and obligation records remain authoritative.

## 2. Two stages

### Preliminary checklist

A requester MAY start with a plain-language description before a complete Proposed Use exists. A preliminary checklist identifies known facts, assumptions and missing information across the five SLATE dimensions. Possible approvals and conditions remain labelled as potential until verified.

A routine use that matches a current standing approval follows the [Minimum-Overhead Profile](MINIMUM_OVERHEAD_PROFILE.md) five-question confirmation instead of creating a new preliminary checklist. A changed or uncertain answer generates only the items needed to resolve the affected gate.

This stage MUST NOT issue a permission outcome or imply that every applicable authority has been found. Formal evaluation still requires a valid, confirmed Proposed Use.

### Evidence-backed checklist

After assessment, the checklist is derived from the current decision snapshot. It shows the decision outcome and the unresolved information, approvals, prohibitions and conditions that govern the next action. Satisfied and non-applicable gates remain available in the linked decision rather than being repeated as checklist items.

When facts or scope change, the implementation reassesses the Proposed Use and regenerates the checklist from the successor decision. Rights conclusions remain in the decision record.

## 3. Required output

A saved checklist MUST identify its stage, creation time, owner, bounded use case and input references. An evidence-backed checklist MUST also identify the decision ID and version.

Each actionable item contains:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Item ID and kind | A stable reference within the checklist; `information`, `approval`, `condition` or `prohibition`. |
| Question or action | One answerable question, bounded approval request, testable action or blocked activity. |
| Scope and basis | The affected gate, material/person/operation/authority as relevant, and the rule, evidence or assumption that created the item. |
| Owner and authority | The coordinating role and, for approvals, the authority that must decide. |
| Evidence and timing | What resolves the item and when it is needed. |
| Dependencies | Earlier items that determine applicability or resolution. |
| Authoritative status | A link to the controlled fact, approval, obligation or successor decision that records resolution. |

Implementations MAY present these fields in a document, sheet or interface. Version 0.2 defines no checklist JSON exchange schema.

## 4. Generation rules

1. Map the request to the five dimensions and ask scope-defining questions first.
2. Label supplied facts, assumptions and generated suggestions clearly. Only verified applicable requirements are confirmed.
3. Derive evidence-backed items from all unresolved gate findings. A higher-severity headline MUST NOT hide other blockers.
4. Keep independent authorities separate. Duplicate items may be consolidated only within one authority's recorded scope.
5. Link resolved work to controlled evidence, approval or obligation records, then reassess. A checked box or generated answer supplies no authority by itself.
6. Show prohibitions as blockers. Amendments and exceptions follow Decision Specification §6.5.

An AI-assisted implementation MUST link generated requirements to their basis, respect evidence access controls and distinguish generated wording from verified findings and exercised authority.

## 5. Worked example: client-show voices for an original production

### Starting from a description

> We want to use dialogue from an old client show to train lip sync for our own new series.

A preliminary checklist asks:

1. Which recordings, performers, productions and models are involved?
2. Which client, contributor, model and collective authorities govern them?
3. What will the system learn, generate and retain?
4. Who will use the model and outputs, and will either leave the destination production?
5. Which approved tool, account and configuration will be used?

Any approval routes remain potential until verified records establish that they apply.

### Using the existing assessment

The [cross-show decision](../examples/cross-show-lipsync.decision.json) produces the following compact evidence-backed checklist. The decision and its [frozen bundle](../examples/cross-show-lipsync.bundle.json) contain the full gate findings and evidence versions.

- **Proposal:** USE-HH-MM-001 version 2.0
- **Decision:** DECISION-HH-MM-001 version 2.0 — Hold: approval required
- **Already established:** the material and model inventories, base-model authority and private tenant configuration are verified within the assessed scope.

| ID / kind | Question or action | Basis and scope | Owner / authority | Evidence and timing | Depends on |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 / information | Verify learned capability, memorisation risk, access, retention and deletion. | Operation finding; retained model | ML lead | Technical statement before final review | — |
| A1 / approval | Obtain authority for the recordings, training, retained model and use on *Moss & Moon*. | Source and destination findings | Business and Legal Affairs / StreamWave Kids | Scoped written authority before processing | Q1 |
| A2 / approval | Obtain Ava's purpose-specific agreement. | Contributor finding; Ava's rider | Business and Legal Affairs / Ava Lin | Executed agreement before processing | Q1, A4 |
| A3 / approval | Resolve Mateo's restriction and create a separate consent request if required. | Contributor finding; Mateo's rider | Business and Legal Affairs / authorised rights reviewer | Scoped finding before processing | Q1, A4 |
| A4 / approval | Establish collective coverage separately for both performers. | Contributor finding; two collective instruments | Business and Legal Affairs / rights and labour reviewer | Individual coverage findings before contributor agreements | Q1 |
| A5 / approval | Establish the privacy basis and contributor process for secondary use. | Privacy finding | Privacy owner / privacy authority | Verified basis and process before processing | Q1, A2–A4 |

All listed items are unresolved at issue; the linked decision is their current authoritative record. Each resolution adds a link to its controlled evidence or successor decision. Q1 bounds the approval requests; agreement review can proceed in parallel. Verified responses update controlled records and produce a successor assessment.

## 6. Acceptance cases

A conforming checklist implementation demonstrates that:

- a description-only request produces explicit unknowns and potential approval routes without a permission outcome;
- the evidence-backed view includes every unresolved gate finding and preserves independent authorities;
- scope-defining questions precede dependent approvals;
- prohibitions remain visible with other unresolved work;
- resolution links to controlled evidence, approval or obligation records;
- changed scope or evidence produces reassessment and a checklist derived from the successor decision; and
- an unidentified or unverified provider-hosted or on-premises model produces an explicit information item rather than inheriting tool approval.
